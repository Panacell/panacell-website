01

Stem Cells

Wharton’s jelly MSC platform

Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) can differentiate into several cell types and release signalling molecules into the medium they grow in. We culture MSCs derived from the Wharton’s jelly of the human umbilical cord, and study adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs).

The culture extract developed by our research team is officially registered in the International Nomenclature of Cosmetic Ingredients (INCI), giving it a standard name for cosmetic ingredient labelling internationally.

Cell source
Human umbilical cord Wharton’s jelly · Adipose tissue
Output
INCI-registered stem cell culture extract
Applications
Cosmetic ingredients · Regenerative medicine research
02

Exosomes

Patented exosome isolation

Exosomes are vesicles of roughly 30–150 nm that cells release to carry proteins, lipids and RNA to other cells. Their usefulness depends on how many can be recovered, and how cleanly.

Our patented method collects the medium produced while NK, NKT and T cells are co-cultured, and isolates up to 4×10¹¹ exosome particles per mL from it by filtration. The patent was registered on 7 August 2024.

4×1011exosome particles per mL
Patent
Exosome manufacturing method, registered August 2024
Applications filed
Three, for cosmetic compositions containing exosomes made with our methods

03

Immune Cells

NK and DC-NK-T immune cells

Natural killer (NK) cells are innate immune cells that recognise and remove abnormal cells without prior sensitisation. We expand NK cells in culture, and grow DC-NK-T combined immune cells, in which dendritic cells, NK cells and T cells are cultured together.

The medium from these co-cultures is also the starting material for our exosome process, so the two platforms share one supply.

Cell types
NK · NKT · T cells · Dendritic cells
Research
Programme to study NK cells, exosomes and adipose-derived stem cells for long COVID (2022)
04

Cell Processing

Licensed cell processing

Cell culture and processing take place in a facility licensed by Korea’s Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) since 2021. The licence is required to supply cells for advanced regenerative medicine in Korea.

We work with a partner clinic designated as an advanced regenerative medicine institution in 2024, covering the path from cell storage to culture and application.
